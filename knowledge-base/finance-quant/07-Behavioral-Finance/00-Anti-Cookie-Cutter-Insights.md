**Purpose**: Catalog non-obvious lessons that challenge conventional trading wisdom. Every insight here should contradict a "common sense" assumption that fails in practice.

## Insight #1: Demand vs. Supply Absorption

> **Buying the strongest mover without a tightness filter = buying extension. The screener identifies demand; tightness confirms supply absorption. They are separate signals.**

**Why it matters**: Most traders equate "highest momentum" with "best setup." But the stock ranked #1 by momentum strength is often the most extended and the least actionable. The momentum screener and the tightness filter measure fundamentally different things:
- **Momentum** = where institutional demand is flowing
- **Tightness** = whether selling pressure has been absorbed and price is compressed

Both signals must align for a high-probability setup. Using only one produces predictable losses.

**Counter-intuitive element**: A rank-1 momentum candidate with tightness = poor setup. A rank-30 candidate with perfect tightness = excellent setup. Ranking is demand-discovery, not trade-selection.

## Insight #2: Volume Dry-Up is Ambiguous Without Context

> **Low volume means nothing by itself. It can signal "selling exhausted" or "nobody cares." The preceding trend decides which.**

**Why it matters**: Many traders see contracting volume and assume accumulation. But without a sustained uptrend preceding the dry-up, low volume indicates the stock has no institutional interest and is dead, not coiling. Only dry-up within an uptrend = supply absorption.

---

## Insight #3: Threshold Calibration is Regime-Dependent

> **Fixed extension thresholds (1.10, 1.20, 1.30) are always wrong in regimes different from where they were calibrated.**

**Why it matters**: Thresholds that produce 30 candidates in a bull market produce 3 in a bear market. The correct response to zero candidates is not "lower the thresholds" — it's "no setups exist right now, stand aside."
